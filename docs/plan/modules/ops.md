# Module `ops`: dashboards, retention, reporting, imports, privacy and AI insights

Owner package: `ops`. Code lives in `packages/db/src/schema/ops.ts` and `ops.views.ts`, `packages/core/src/ops/`, `apps/web/modules/ops/`, `apps/web/app/(admin)/admin/{dashboard,reports,imports,privacy}/`, `apps/web/app/[locale]/(portal)/account/privacy/`, `packages/integrations/src/ads/` (R3), `packages/email/src/templates/ops/`, `packages/core/src/i18n/messages/<locale>/ops.json`, `packages/db/src/seed/ops.ts`, `packages/db/src/sql/0101_ai_views_ops.sql` (R4), `docs/runbooks/data-migration.md`. Blueprint sections 20, 22, 23, 33, 34, 38, 39, 40, 48, 53, 62, 63, 66, plus requirements found in 3, 6, 9, 15, 18, 21, 31, 37, 43, 47, 49, 56, 59, 60, 68, 70, 76, 77, 87 (scenario 7) and 89.

## 1. Purpose and release mapping

`ops` turns the facts other modules write into numbers and lists people act on. It also runs the data workflows that touch every module: the migration import, data exports, privacy requests and the retention sweep. Rules come first (blueprint section 2): every list and score here is deterministic SQL. Claude is used only in Release 4, for questions and the weekly summary, always over a read-only, non-sensitive schema.

| Release | What ships in `ops` |
| --- | --- |
| **V1 (weeks 1-6)** | `person_stats` cache (nightly at 04:00, every 5 minutes for recently active people, on demand) with historical lifetime value; `b2c_stage` derivation; `person.inactive_days` threshold events (30, 60, 90, 180); `v_person_profile` (the only view the segment compiler targets); `v_retention_candidates`, `v_revenue_daily`, `v_no_show_report`, `v_abandoned_checkouts` as views; the **staff action dashboard** (`/admin/dashboard`) with the V1 cards and a Today / This week toggle (the webinar-leads card reads registrations imported from the current webinar tool until R2); **basic reporting** (`/admin/reports/revenue`: revenue daily, weekly, monthly, YTD with previous-period comparison, VAT included or excluded, sales by product kind and channel, prepaid credits outstanding from credits' `v_credit_liability`), so "reports reflect the transaction" (acceptance V1); **data migration** (CSV upload, mapping, dry run with report, chunked processing, duplicate handling by `lower(email)`, opening package balances as `import` grants with a reconciliation report BEAM signs off, cut-over checklist, real runs in production only after platform's go-live gate is complete, runbook `docs/runbooks/data-migration.md`); **exports** (CSV per entity and the full data export); **privacy requests** (export, erasure, restriction, rectification, objection) with scenario 7; `retention_policies` and the nightly `privacy.retention_sweep`; DPIA register; `ops.daily_digest` Slack message |
| **Release 2 (weeks 9-10)** | **Retention engine** screen and the weekly list "these are the 25 customers requiring attention this week" with one-click task creation; **B2C pipeline board** by `b2c_stage`; **no-show and cancellation report**; **abandoned checkout report** with recovery revenue; capacity utilisation report over booking's V1 view `v_session_utilisation`; action-dashboard cards that need R2 data (native webinar registrations added to the webinar-leads card, shared-package abuse review from credits' `v_sharing_review`); webinar fields in `v_person_profile`; native webinar data in the scenario 7 spec |
| **Release 3 (weeks 11-12)** | **Management dashboard** in full (customers new, active, returning, dormant, reactivated; sales mix; retention rates; B2B pipeline from crm R3 opportunities); **campaign attribution** (first-touch from `persons.acquisition`, linear multi-touch from UTM touches and orders, and the per-campaign funnel of blueprint 33: lead, webinar, first visit, package, repeat purchase, membership, lifetime value); **Meta and Google Ads read-only spend import** into `ad_spend_daily` with cost per lead, CAC, ROAS, revenue and lifetime value by campaign; **marketing dashboards** (leads, registration and attendance rates, lead-to-visit, visit-to-purchase, email performance, reactivation revenue, webinar and assessment conversion, abandoned-cart recovery); email and webinar performance summaries; B2B proposals card on the action dashboard |
| **Release 4 (weeks 13-14)** | **Custom dashboards** from the KPI catalogue; **natural-language questions** answered by Claude over the read-only `ai` schema as `beam_ai_ro`; **weekly management summary**; **churn and repurchase indicators** and predicted value; **customer opportunity identification** |
| **Later (out of scope)** | Predictive analytics beyond the rule-based indicators (trained models, forecasting), AI ranking that acts without a human, automated B2B prospecting analytics, voice AI reporting, gamification metrics, a native mobile dashboard, ad creation in Meta or Google, Google Analytics import |

The proposal places the retention engine, the B2C pipeline and the no-show and abandoned-checkout reports in Release 2 and the management dashboard in Release 3. The views behind them exist from V1 because the V1 action dashboard reads them and because the architecture lists them as V1 views. Their screens stay behind feature flags until their release: `ff_ops_retention` (R2), `ff_ops_marketing` (R3), `ff_ops_ai` (R4).

### 2.4 Views (`ops.views.ts`, Drizzle `pgView(...).as(sql\`...\`)`)

Thresholds come from `settings.retention_defaults` (Zod `RetentionDefaults` in `packages/core/src/ops/retention-defaults.ts`; seeded): `{ visit_drop_ratio: 0.5, baseline_min_visits: 3, nearly_finished_pct: 80, expiry_days: 30, finished_no_repurchase_min_days: 14, finished_no_repurchase_max_days: 120, high_value_revenue_cents: 100000, vip_tag_key: "vip", high_value_inactive_days: 60, renewal_days: 30, never_booked_days: 14, unused_days: 45, weekly_list_size: 25, task_cooldown_days: 14, new_lead_followup_hours: 48, new_lead_window_days: 14, webinar_lead_window_days: 14, membership_expiring_days: 14, guest_abuse_distinct_guests: 3, guest_abuse_window_days: 30, stage_retention_days: 90, stage_lost_days: 365, stage_engaged_window_days: 90, no_show_pattern_count: 3, no_show_pattern_days: 90, dashboard_week_horizon_days: 7 }`. **`v_retention_candidates`** (V1). `union all` of eight rule blocks over people in `v_person_profile` with `is_tester = false`. Columns: `person_id, reason text, score integer, ref_type text, ref_id uuid, detail jsonb`. Rules (all times `now()`):

| reason | Rule | ref | Base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `visiting_less` | `visits_prev_180d >= baseline_min_visits` and `visits_last_60d / 60.0 < visit_drop_ratio * visits_prev_180d / 180.0` and `has_future_booking = false` | person | 30 |
| `package_nearly_finished` | grant `status = 'active'`, `units_granted > 0`, used share `(units_granted - remaining) * 100 / units_granted >= nearly_finished_pct`, `remaining >= 1` | grant | 35 |
| `package_finished_no_repurchase` | grant `status = 'exhausted'`, last `consume` entry between `finished_no_repurchase_min_days` and `max_days` ago, no grant of the same `credit_kind` created after it, no active membership | grant | 40 |
| `package_expiring` | grant `status = 'active'`, `remaining > 0`, `expires_at` within `expiry_days` | grant | 45 |
| `high_value_inactive` | (`revenue_cents >= high_value_revenue_cents` or tag `vip_tag_key`) and `days_since_activity >= high_value_inactive_days` and `has_future_booking = false` | person | 50 |
| `membership_renewal_due` | membership `status in ('active','past_due')` and `current_period_end_at` within `renewal_days` | membership | 40 |
| `purchased_never_booked` | a grant with `source = 'purchase'` created more than `never_booked_days` ago and the person has no booking at all | grant | 30 |
| `unused_sessions` | grant `status = 'active'`, `remaining > 0`, no `consume` entry on it in `unused_days`, created more than `unused_days` ago, `has_future_booking = false` | grant | 25 |

`score = base + least(30, revenue_cents / 20000) + urgency`, where urgency is `greatest(0, 20 - days_until_expiry_or_period_end)` for `package_expiring` and `membership_renewal_due` (plus 10 when `memberships.cancel_at` is set), `least(20, days_since_activity / 10)` for `visiting_less`, `high_value_inactive`, `unused_sessions`, and 0 otherwise. `detail` carries `{grant_id, remaining, units_granted, expires_at, product_name}` or `{membership_id, period_end_at, cancel_at}` or `{visits_last_60d, visits_prev_180d, days_since_activity}`. Remaining units come from credits' `v_credit_grant_balances.remaining_units` (with `base_units` as the size of the package and `last_used_at` as the last consume); "remaining" and "units_granted" in the table above mean those two columns.

**`v_revenue_daily`** (V1):

```sql
select (o.paid_at at time zone 'Europe/Brussels')::date as day_on, o.channel, p.kind as product_kind,
       count(distinct o.id)::int as orders, sum(ol.line_total_cents)::int as gross_cents,
       sum(ol.vat_cents)::int as vat_cents, 0 as refunds_cents, 0 as refunds_vat_cents
from orders o join order_lines ol on ol.order_id = o.id join products p on p.id = ol.product_id
left join persons pe on pe.id = o.person_id
where o.paid_at is not null and o.status in ('paid','partially_refunded','refunded')
  and coalesce(pe.is_tester, false) = false
group by 1, 2, 3
union all
select (r.created_at at time zone 'Europe/Brussels')::date, o.channel, 'refund', 0, 0, 0,
       sum(r.amount_cents)::int, coalesce(sum(cn.vat_cents), 0)::int
from refunds r join payments pm on pm.id = r.payment_id join orders o on o.id = pm.order_id
left join persons pe on pe.id = o.person_id
left join invoices cn on cn.id = r.credit_note_id
where r.status = 'refunded' and coalesce(pe.is_tester, false) = false group by 1, 2
```

Net revenue = `gross_cents - refunds_cents` (VAT included, the default); net revenue excluding VAT = `gross_cents - vat_cents - (refunds_cents - refunds_vat_cents)`; credit-note VAT is taken from `invoices.vat_cents` (stored positive on credit notes by finance). Company orders (`person_id` null) count through the left join. Tester persons' orders are excluded.

## 3. Business rules

Each rule has a test named after its number (`R<n>` in the test title).

**Person stats and lifetime value**
1. `revenue_cents` = sum of `orders.total_cents` with status `paid`, `partially_refunded` or `refunded` for the person, minus `refunds.amount_cents` with status `refunded` on those orders, plus `imported_history.amount_cents` of kind `purchase`. Orders of a company (`company_id` set, `person_id` null) never count for a person.
2. `purchases` counts those orders plus imported purchases. `avg_order_cents = round(revenue_cents / purchases)` half up; 0 when `purchases = 0`.
3. A visit is a booking with status `attended` on a session of kind `day` or `night`, or an imported `visit`. A booking with status `no_show` is never a visit, never sets `last_visit_at` and never moves `b2c_stage` forward (blueprint 48). Guest bookings count for the guest, not the package owner.
4. `next_booking_at` is the earliest future `starts_at` of the person's `reserved` or `confirmed` bookings, including guest bookings for them.
5. The nightly `ops.refresh_person_stats` recomputes every non-erased, non-merged person in chunks of 2,000 ordered by `person_id` with one set-based `insert ... on conflict (person_id) do update`. Running it twice gives identical rows apart from `computed_at`.
6. `ops.refresh_recent_stats` (every 5 minutes) recomputes the people with an `activity_events` row of a kind in `STATS_KINDS` (`order.paid`, `order.refunded`, `booking.confirmed`, `booking.cancelled`, `booking.attended`, `booking.no_show`, `credits.*`, `membership.*`, `form.completed`, `email.clicked`, `webinar.attended`, `ops.history_imported`) since `ops_state.stats_cursor`, at most 1,000 people per run, then advances the cursor. The run starts with `select ... from ops_state where key = 'stats_cursor' for update skip locked`; when the row is locked by a slower previous run it exits at once, so two runs never move the cursor backwards.
7. `refreshPersonStats(tx, personId)` recomputes one person synchronously; staff trigger it with "Recalculate" on the person page.

**B2C stage** (`computeB2cStage(stats, status, signals)` in `packages/core/src/ops/stage.ts`, first match wins)
8. `member` when `v_person_status.status = 'member'` (this honours `status_override`).
9. `lost` when `purchases >= 1`, the last visit or purchase is more than `stage_lost_days` (default 365) days ago and there is no future booking.
10. `retention` when `purchases >= 1`, the last visit or purchase is more than `stage_retention_days` (default 90) days ago and there is no future booking. A person who comes back after that gap moves to `active` or `repeat` again and counts as reactivated (rule 36).
11. `repeat` when `purchases >= 2`. `active` when `purchases = 1` and `visits >= 2`. `first_purchase` when `purchases = 1`.
12. `first_visit` when `visits >= 1` and `purchases = 0` (a guest or free visit). `experienced` when `purchases = 0`, `visits = 0` and the person attended an event session or a webinar (live, replay or on demand).
13. `engaged_lead` when `purchases = 0` and in the last `stage_engaged_window_days` (default 90) days the person completed a form, clicked an email, registered for a webinar or made a booking. Otherwise `lead`.
14. When the stage changes, the job writes `b2c_stage_changed_at` and an activity event `ops.stage_changed { from, to }` with `dedupe_key = stage:<person_id>:<to>:<YYYY-MM-DD>`. Staff cannot set the stage by hand; it is derived only. Status corrections go through crm's `status_override`.
14a. Blueprint 20 names the top stage "Member / High-Value Customer". The stage `member` stays tied to `v_person_status` (so member pricing and the stage never disagree); a high-value non-member keeps their commercial stage and carries `is_high_value = true` in `v_person_profile`. The pipeline board labels the `member` column "Member / high value" and shows high-value people of other stages with a badge and a filter.

**Inactivity events**
15. Inactivity applies to people with `purchases >= 1` or `visits >= 1`. The anchor is the later of `last_visit_at` and `last_purchase_at`.
16. For thresholds 30, 60, 90 and 180 days, the nightly job emits `person.inactive_days { days, anchor_at, has_future_booking }` when `anchor + days` falls within the last 3 days (Brussels dates). `dedupe_key = person:<id>:inactive:<days>:<anchor date>`. A new visit or purchase moves the anchor and re-arms every threshold. After the cut-over import nobody receives a burst of old thresholds, and a missed night is caught up for 3 days.
17. When two thresholds fall inside the same 3-day window, only the highest is emitted.
18. The event carries `has_future_booking`; seeded inactivity automations filter on it (blueprint 21). `ops` never sends email itself.

**Retention engine** (R2 screen, V1 view)
19. A person appears in `v_retention_candidates` once per matching reason and grant; erased, merged, restricted and tester persons never appear.
20. The weekly list is built every Monday at 06:10 Brussels into `retention_lists` for that week. One row per person with their highest score (`primary_reason`) and all their reasons. It excludes people with an open or done crm task with `source = 'retention'` created in the last `task_cooldown_days`. It keeps the top `weekly_list_size` (default 25) by score, then revenue, then `person_id`. A rebuild in the same week replaces only items with no `task_id` and no `dismissed_at`. The build first does `insert into retention_lists ... on conflict (week_start_on) do nothing`, then `select ... for update` on that row, so the Monday job and a manual rebuild never interleave. After the Monday build, `notifySlack` posts one line ("Weekly retention list ready: 25 people, 9 packages expiring", counts per primary reason and the link, no names) with fingerprint `retention-list:<week>`; this is how management "receives" the list (blueprint 22).
21. "Create task" on an item creates one crm task (`kind = 'follow_up'`, `source = 'retention'`, `priority = high` when score >= 80 else `normal`, `due_at` = next business day 10:00 Brussels, `ref_type = 'retention_list_item'`, title from the catalogue key `ops.retention.task_title.<reason>` in the owner's locale) and stores `task_id`. The action locks the item `for update` first, so a second click (or a second tab) returns the existing task. "Next business day" is `nextBusinessDay(at, businessHours)` in `packages/core/src/ops/business-days.ts`: the first following day that is open in booking's `settings.business_hours`, is not a booking closed-day exception, and is not a Belgian public holiday (`BE_PUBLIC_HOLIDAYS(year)`: 1 January, Easter Monday, 1 May, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, 21 July, 15 August, 1 November, 11 November, 25 December, Easter computed with the anonymous Gregorian algorithm). "Create all tasks" does the same for every item without a task, in one transaction.
22. Dismissing an item requires a reason (`not_relevant`, `already_contacted`, `do_not_contact`, `other`) and keeps the row.

**Action dashboard** (each card: a count, the first 10 rows, a link to the full list, and "Create task" per row)
22a. "Create task" on a card row (`createTaskFromCard`) locks the `persons` row `for update`, then looks for an open crm task with the same `person_id`, `ref_type = 'action_card:<card>'` and `ref_id`; it returns that task when present, else creates one (`kind`: `payment` for `failed_payments`, `proposal` for `b2b_proposals`, `review` for `shared_package_abuse`, `follow_up` otherwise; `source = 'retention'` for the retention-reason cards, `manual` for the others; due next business day 10:00).
22b. The dashboard has a Today / This week toggle (blueprint 38 "daily / weekly"). Today shows the windows of rules 23-32 as written. This week widens every date horizon by `dashboard_week_horizon_days` (7): tasks due by the end of the ISO week, memberships and packages expiring within the window plus 7 days, and new leads and webinar leads of the last 7 days. The toggle is a URL parameter (`?view=week`), not a saved setting.
23. `packages_nearing_completion`: rows of reason `package_nearly_finished`.
24. `vip_inactive`: rows of reason `high_value_inactive` (default more than 60 days).
25. `new_leads_follow_up`: people with status `lead` created between `new_lead_window_days` days and `new_lead_followup_hours` hours ago, `acquisition_source` not `import`, no booking and no crm task referencing them.
26. `b2b_proposals` (R3): crm opportunities in stage `proposal` or `negotiation` whose `next_action_at` is past or null. Hidden without `crm.b2b`.
27. `memberships_expiring`: memberships `active` with `cancel_at` within `membership_expiring_days`, or `past_due`, or `current_period_end_at` within that window and no `valid` mandate.
28. `failed_payments`: payments `failed` or `expired` in the last 7 days whose order is not paid later, plus payments `pending` or `open` for more than 15 minutes.
29. `webinar_leads_not_booked`: leads registered for a webinar that started in the last `webinar_lead_window_days` with no booking made after registration. In V1 the source is `imported_history` rows of kind `webinar_registration` (from the current webinar tool, proposal section 3; webinar start from `data.webinar_starts_at`, else `occurred_at`); R2 adds native `webinar_registrations` with `webinars.starts_at` and de-duplicates by person.
30. `unused_packages_expiring`: rows of reason `package_expiring`.
31. `shared_package_abuse` (R2): rows of credits' `v_sharing_review` (more than 3 distinct guests on one grant in 30 days, or one guest on 3 or more owners' grants; already-reviewed rows are excluded by credits), plus `waitlist_entries` with `is_flagged_duplicate = true` and state `waiting` or `offered`. Reviewing happens on credits' screen; the card links there.
32. `overdue_tasks`: open crm tasks with `due_at < now()` (the user's own; all with `tasks.all`).
33. Counts are computed live (no cache); the page must render under 1.5 s with 10,000 persons in the seed.

**Management dashboard and reports** (periods in Brussels time)
34. Periods: day (vs previous day), ISO week Monday-Sunday (vs previous week), month-to-date (vs the same number of days of the previous month), YTD (vs the same span of the previous year). The comparison shows absolute and percentage change; percentage is blank when the previous value is 0.
35. Revenue tiles use `v_revenue_daily` net revenue, VAT included by default with a toggle "excl. VAT" (rule in 2.4). Sales mix groups by product kind: `package`, `night`, `session`, `membership`, `item` (products), `ticket` (events), `voucher`, and by channel (`portal`, `site`, `admin`, `pos`, `link`, `recurring`).
35a. Prepaid credits outstanding (deferred revenue, V1 on the revenue report): today's `v_credit_liability` (remaining units and value per credit kind, VAT treatment and rate) and, for a past period end, credits' `getCreditLiabilityAsOf(db, at)`. Sold packages and vouchers count as revenue at sale (architecture section 6); this block shows what is still owed in sessions, nights and euro credit so the accountant can book the liability. It reads credits' figures and computes nothing itself.
36. Customers (R3): `new` = first purchase in the period; `active` = a visit or purchase in the 90 days before period end; `returning` = active in the period and also active before it; `dormant` = customers whose last visit or purchase is 90-365 days before period end; `reactivated` = a visit or purchase in the period after a gap of more than 90 days.
37. Retention (R3): repeat purchase rate = customers with `purchases >= 2` / customers; package completion = grants that became `exhausted` / grants that became `exhausted` or `expired` in the period; reactivation rate = reactivated / dormant at period start; membership renewal rate = paid renewals / renewals due in the period.
38. B2B (R3): pipeline value = sum `value_cents` of open opportunities (not `won`, `lost`); weighted = sum `value_cents * probability_pct / 100`; qualified = stages `qualified` to `negotiation`; proposals = stage `proposal`; conversion = won / (won + lost) closed in the period; B2B revenue = paid orders with `company_id`.
39. No-show report (R2): totals per status (`cancelled`, `late_cancelled`, `no_show`), by customer (count, rate = no-shows / (attended + no-shows)), by template, by weekday x hour, value and credits forfeited; a customer with `no_show_pattern_count` (3) or more no-shows in `no_show_pattern_days` (90) is flagged as a pattern. Rows with `is_session_cancelled = true` (BEAM cancelled the session) are shown apart and never count in a customer's figures or patterns.
40. Abandoned checkouts (R2): abandoned count, recovered count (a paid `recovered_order_id`), recovery rate, recovery revenue, failure share.
41. Every report excludes tester persons and has a CSV download of exactly the rows on screen (audited as `data.exported`).

**Imports** (blueprint 53, risks 7 and 10)
42. Upload: CSV (UTF-8, optional BOM, delimiter `,` `;` or tab), max 50 MB, sent in 3 MB parts to `/admin/imports/upload`. The first 20 rows and the header are shown for mapping.
43. Required mapping per kind (`IMPORT_TARGETS`): `persons` needs `email` or (`phone` and `last_name`); `suppressions` needs `email`; `consents` needs `email` and a status column; `packages` needs `email`, `units_remaining`, `expires_on` and a product; `memberships` needs `email`, `product_sku`, `period_end_on`; `bookings` needs `email` and `session_starts_at`; `history` needs `email`, `occurred_at`, `history_kind`, `external_id`; `companies` needs `name`; `tags` needs `email` and a tag (column or option).
43a. Import states (`imports.status`), each transition tested: `uploaded` → `dry_run` (`startDryRun`: `run_seq + 1`, parse and process in dry mode; `finished_at` set by `import.finalise`); `dry_run` → `dry_run` (mapping changed and a new dry run started); `dry_run` → `running` (`startImport`, only when the finished dry run has the same mapping hash); `failed` → `running` (`startImport` again after a run failure: `run_seq + 1`, only `pending` rows are processed); `running` → `done` (finalise); `uploaded` or `dry_run` → `failed` (parse `PermanentError`); `running` → `failed` (a chunk fails on its last attempt: the handler sets `failed` with `report.error` before rethrowing); `uploaded`, `dry_run` or `running` → `cancelled` (`cancelImport`; running chunks read the status at the start of each chunk and exit; rows already imported stay and the report says "partial"). `done` and `cancelled` are final. A parse failure is final for that import: staff fix the file and create a new import.
44a. In production, `startImport` (not dry runs) is refused with `conflict` "Complete the go-live gate first" while platform's `isGoLiveGateComplete(tx)` returns false (platform rule PL-77). Outside production it is not checked.
44. A dry run processes every row and writes `dry_run_status` and `message` but changes nothing outside `import_rows` and `imports`. Its report lists: rows per outcome, new versus matched persons, duplicates inside the file, invalid emails, unknown products, suppressed addresses whose consent was skipped, and for packages the reconciliation. A real run is allowed only after a dry run of the same mapping (mapping hash stored in `report.mapping_hash`).
45. Matching: a row matches an existing person by `lower(trim(email))`. Without email it matches by E.164 phone plus `lower(last_name)`; with no match a new person is created. A second row with the same email in the file is merged into the first (`updated`). An existing `import_external_ids` row matches first.
46. Matched persons are updated only in empty fields unless `options.overwrite = true`; `locale`, `acquisition` and `created_at` are never overwritten. New persons get `acquisition = { source: 'import', campaign: <import name> }` and `locale` from the row or `defaultLocale`. The persons applier writes only through crm's `upsertPersonFromImport(tx, ctx, row, importId)` (which owns the `lower(email)` uniqueness and race handling); `ops` decides the match (rule 45) and passes `onlyEmpty = !options.overwrite`.
47. Imports never remove or override a suppression. A `consents` row with status granted for an address suppressed for `all` or for that purpose is `skipped` with message `suppressed`. Suppression rows import through crm only with kind `suppressions`. A consent import appends `consent_records` rows (method `import`, `recorded_at` and `wording_version` from the file or options) and never edits or deletes an earlier row, so consent history is kept (acceptance "consent history is retained"). An import never creates newsletter consent from a column that is not a consent column (for example a Mailchimp "member rating").
47a. Every importer skips a row whose `sha256(lower(trim(email)))` has a suppression with reason `erased` (message `erased`), so an old tool's export never brings an erased person back (proposal scenario 7). Imports of source `webinar_tool` follow webinar's V1 stopgap rules 1-7 (webinar spec section 4): mapping presets `webinargeek_registrants`, `webinargeek_attendees`, `zoom_registrants`, `zoom_attendees`; new persons get `acquisition.source = 'webinar'` instead of `import`; no consent or suppression rows even when an opt-in column exists; `external_id = <provider>:<external_webinar_id>:<first 16 hex of sha256(lower(email))>`; weekly re-imports add nothing twice. Mapping presets for `simplybook`, `lightspeed` and `mailchimp` exports live in the same constant `IMPORT_PRESETS` (`packages/core/src/ops/import-presets.ts`).
47b. `ops.history_imported` events are facts about the past: automation never uses them as triggers (an automation definition whose trigger is `ops.history_imported` fails validation in automation's publish check), so no reminder, review request or follow-up fires for imported history.
48. Opening package balances: each `packages` row creates one `credit_grants` row (`source = 'import'`, `units_granted = units_remaining`, `expires_at` from the file, `starts_at` = import time) and one `grant` ledger entry with key `import:<import_id>:row:<row_number>`. The reconciliation report lists per credit kind and per product: rows, units in the file, units granted, the difference, and every row whose post-import balance in `v_credit_grant_balances` differs from the file. The import stays `done` but the cut-over step `packages_reconciled` can only be ticked after an owner or management user signs off (`signed_off_at`).
49. Memberships import as `active` with `current_period_end_at` from the file and no mandate; the credits module then sends its payment-link renewal flow. Future bookings import only for materialised sessions (`booking.importBooking`), with `source = 'import'`, status `reserved` or `confirmed` and no credit consumed (the opening balance already excludes them, rule 48); a row without a matching session is `error` "no session at that time". Past bookings import as `history` rows (`visit`, `no_show`, `cancellation`, `event_attendance`), not through booking's `importHistoricalBooking`, so no booking events, review requests or ledger entries fire for the past (decision 23).
50. Every imported row writes `import_external_ids` when the file has an external id, and every `history` row writes an activity event `ops.history_imported` with `occurred_at` set to the original time and `dedupe_key = import:<source>:<kind>:<external_id>`.
51. Chunks: 500 rows per `import.process_chunk` job, each in its own transaction, enqueuing the next offset. A row error marks that row `error` and never aborts the chunk. A job crash retries the chunk; rows already `imported` or `updated` are skipped on retry. Every job key of an import carries `run_seq` (4.4), so a second dry run or a resume never collides with the keys of an earlier run.
52. Raw uploaded parts get `files.expires_at` = 30 days after the import finishes; `import_rows` are purged after 30 days.
53. Cut-over checklist `CUTOVER_STEPS` (14), in order: `go_live_gate_complete`, `suppressions_imported`, `persons_imported`, `consents_imported`, `tags_imported`, `companies_imported`, `history_imported`, `packages_imported`, `packages_reconciled`, `memberships_imported`, `future_bookings_imported`, `stats_recomputed`, `old_tools_frozen`, `dns_switched`. `go_live_gate_complete` mirrors platform's `isGoLiveGateComplete()` (which covers blueprint 77: retention table agreed, export and deletion tested, and the rest); the next ten are ticked automatically when a matching `done` import exists (and, for `packages_reconciled`, sign-off); `stats_recomputed` is ticked when an `ops.refresh_person_stats` run finished after the last import; `old_tools_frozen` and `dns_switched` are ticked by hand. The runbook `docs/runbooks/data-migration.md` (ops-P4) walks through the same steps with the export settings for SimplyBook, Lightspeed and Mailchimp, the column mapping per source and the freeze window (old tools read-only from the Friday evening, imports Saturday, DNS Sunday). An import of kind `consents` or `persons` cannot run while `suppressions_imported` is not done unless the user confirms "no suppression source exists" (stored as a note).

**Exports**
54. Entity CSV export (`EXPORT_ENTITIES`: `persons, companies, company_contacts, tags, consents, suppressions, bookings, sessions, orders, order_lines, payments, refunds, invoices, credit_grants, credit_entries, memberships, form_submissions, email_messages, campaigns, tasks, activity_events`) uses fixed column lists that exclude `SENSITIVE_COLUMNS`, password hashes, TOTP and recovery secrets, token hashes and `raw` payloads. Restricted persons are excluded from staff exports.
55. Each export runs in 5,000-row chunks, each chunk one CSV file part (at most 9 MB). The download streams a store-only zip of the parts. Files expire after 7 days.
56. The full data export contains every entity of rule 54 plus every other platform table with the same exclusions, as one CSV per table, and a `README.csv` listing tables, row counts and excluded columns. It needs `persons.export` and `users.manage`. Unlike entity exports it includes restricted persons, with a column `is_processing_restricted`, because it is BEAM's own copy of its data (blueprint 68, 70) and moving storage is not new processing. The complete database copy for exit is the encrypted `pg_dump` of `backup.yml`.
57. Every export writes `audit_log` action `data.exported` with entity, filters and row count.

**Privacy requests** (blueprint 62, scenario 7)
58. Due date is 30 days after receipt. A request over 25 days old and not `done` shows on the action dashboard and in the digest.
59. A customer's request from `/[locale]/account/privacy` is verified by their signed-in session (`verified_at` = now). A staff-entered request needs `privacy.run` to verify, with a verification note. From the portal a customer can request `export`, `erase`, `restrict` and `object` (applied at once, rules 65-66) and `rectify` (field names picked from a list; staff complete it). A system request (rule 69) is verified at creation.
59a. Request states (`privacy_requests.status`), each transition tested: `received` → `verified` (`verifyPrivacyRequest`, or at creation for customer and system requests); `received` or `verified` → `cancelled` (`cancelPrivacyRequest`, `cancelMyErasure` before `scheduled_for_at`); `verified` → `running` (export: at once; erase: `privacy.erase` at `scheduled_for_at` or `runPrivacyRequestNow`); `running` → `done` (`privacy.finalise` or the export job); `running` → `failed` (a local step or the export job fails on its last attempt; external failures never fail the request, rule 63); `failed` → `running` (`runPrivacyRequestNow` increments `run_attempt`, so every job key is new, and reruns from the external steps; every handler is idempotent and the report keeps the latest count per table); `verified` → `done` directly for `restrict` and `object` (applied in the verifying transaction) and for `rectify` (`completeRectification`). `done` and `cancelled` are final.
60. Export: one zip with, per module, `<module>.json` and one CSV per table, built from each `GDPR_REGISTRY` entry's `export(tx, personId)`, plus `sensitive.json` from `readSensitive(ctx, personId, 'privacy_export')`, plus `README.txt` in the person's locale. The README carries what GDPR article 15 requires besides the data: the purposes and legal bases (from the person's consent records and the fixed list of contractual purposes), the categories of recipients (the subprocessor list of proposal section 9, constant `SUBPROCESSORS` in `packages/core/src/ops/privacy-notice.ts`), the retention period per data category (read from `retention_policies`), the source of the data (`persons.acquisition.source`, imports named by source), and the right to complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority (Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit / Autorité de protection des données). The JSON files are the portable, machine-readable copy (article 20). When the zip exceeds 9 MB it is stored in 9 MB parts (`file_id` then `part_file_ids`) and the download streams them in order. The zip is encrypted with AES-256-GCM (`SENSITIVE_DATA_KEY_V1`) before storage, expires after 7 days, and is downloadable only by the subject in the portal or by `privacy.run` staff. The customer gets the email `ops/privacy-export-ready`.
61. Erasure starts at `scheduled_for_at` (24 h after verification); until then the subject or staff can cancel. `privacy.run` staff may start it at once. Warnings (`active_membership`, `credits_remaining`, `future_bookings`, `open_invoices`, `open_orders`) must be acknowledged; nothing blocks erasure except another running request for the person.
62. Erasure order: first external propagation (Resend delete contact, Billit anonymise customer, and revocation of the person's `valid` payment mandates through commerce's `revokeMandatesForPerson(tx, ctx, personId)` so no renewal can be charged afterwards), then module handlers in `ERASE_ORDER = ['webinar','automation','email','forms','booking','credits','commerce','finance','site','ops','crm','platform']` (`site` holds `terms_acceptances` and `cookie_consents`), each in its own transaction under `SET LOCAL beam.allow_rewrite = 'on'`, then the report. `crm` anonymises `persons` and keeps the suppression hash with reason `erased`; `platform` redacts `audit_log`, deletes `ai_requests` and the person's files and disables the customer user.
63. An external call that is still failing on its last attempt is recorded as `failed` in the report, `needs_manual_followup = true`, and the local erasure continues. Each payment provider the person has `payments` with (Mollie, Stripe, Viva) is recorded as `provider_retains` (payment data kept under the provider's own legal obligations; mandates revoked per rule 62), and Anthropic as `provider_retains` (API data retention by the provider for at most 30 days, no deletion endpoint; local `ai_requests` rows are deleted).
63a. `privacy.erase` starts only when no other request of the person is `running`: it locks the `persons` row `for update`, checks, and otherwise throws a `RetryableError` so the job runs again after backoff. An export never runs while an erasure of the same person runs.
64. The report holds only module, table, row counts, actions, legal bases and provider outcomes. The `audit_log` row `privacy.erased` has `entity_id = request id` and `new_value = { rows_total, modules }`, no name or email. The erasure confirmation email `ops/privacy-erasure-scheduled` goes out at verification, because no address exists afterwards. System requests from the retention sweep send no email (the person asked for nothing; the privacy notice on the site states the retention periods).
65. Restriction inserts `processing_restrictions`, calls crm's `restrictProcessing(tx, ctx, personId, true)`, records `withdrawn` consent for every marketing purpose (method `staff`, source `privacy_restriction`), exits all active enrolments through automation with reason `restricted`, and emits `privacy.restricted`. Contractual processing (bookings, payments, invoices) continues. Lifting deletes the row, calls `restrictProcessing(tx, ctx, personId, false)` and never re-grants consent.
66. Objection does the same as restriction for marketing, plus a crm suppression with scope `all` and reason `manual`, and leaves no restriction row (the person may still book).
67. Rectification stores only field names in `rectify_fields`; staff apply the change on the person page (crm audit holds old and new values), then mark the request done, which enqueues finance's Billit customer resync.

**Retention sweep, DPIA, digest**
68. `privacy.retention_sweep` (01:00) runs active policies by `run_order`. Per policy it uses the owning module's `sweep[key]` handler when registered, else the generic handler for allow-listed tables (`created_at < now() - days`, `batch_size` rows per transaction under `SET LOCAL beam.allow_rewrite = 'on'`, self-continuing), else records `no_handler`. `keep` policies are skipped.
69. `marketing_profile` (anonymise) creates a `privacy_requests` row (`kind = erase`, `requested_by = system`, verified and scheduled at once) for each person returned by crm's `listInactiveProfiles(tx, now() - days, 200)` (no activity event, order, booking, login, active grant, active membership or open invoice in `days`), skipping people with a future booking or an open privacy request. At most 200 per night. This is the only policy that erases whole profiles; crm's former key `crm.inactive_profile` is not seeded (decision 41a).
69a. `marketing_contact` (anonymise, 730 days): the same pipeline for leads only (`purchases = 0`, no booking ever, no invoice, no grant), so a marketing contact who never became a customer is removed after 2 years without activity, while customers follow `marketing_profile` (blueprint 63 lists "customer profile" and "marketing contact" separately). It shares the 200-per-night cap with rule 69 (contacts first).
70. A feature listed in `DPIA_GATED` (`sensitive_assessment_processing` for forms publishing a version with `sensitive_field_keys` and for platform's sensitive-AI switch, scenario 6; `behavioural_scoring` for R4 churn indicators and opportunity scores; `ai_customer_ranking` for R4 NL questions that read `ai.retention_candidates` or `ai.customers` churn columns; `watch_time_profiling` for R2 watch-time segment fields) stays off until its `dpia_records.status` is `approved` or `not_required`. `isDpiaApproved(db, key)` is the one check. `b2b_prospecting` and `voice_ai` gate nothing in this build because those features are Later; their rows exist so the register is complete. Setting `approved` or `not_required` needs a `decision_note`; every change is audited.
71. `ops.daily_digest` at 08:00 posts one Slack message: yesterday's sends, bounces and complaints and the bounce rate, `blocked_translation` messages, failed automation runs (`automation_runs.outcome = 'failed'`), failed payments, privacy requests near their due date or needing manual follow-up, imports finished or failed, policies with `no_handler`, ads data age (R3), followed by the sections returned by platform's `getPlatformDigest(tx, day)` (dead jobs, backlog, tick age, webhook and integration errors, AI spend, database and file size, open incidents) and finance's `getFinanceDigest(tx, day)` (Billit failures, open invoices). This covers blueprint 72's monitored failures in one daily view; real-time alerts stay with platform. Ids and counts only. Key `schedule:ops.daily_digest:<minute>`; `ops_state.digest_last_sent_on` prevents a second post the same day.

**Attribution and ads** (R3)
72. First-touch attribution credits 100 % of every paid order to the person's `acquisition` campaign (`direct|none` when absent). Linear multi-touch splits each order equally over the distinct campaign keys in `v_marketing_touches` within 90 days before `paid_at`, falling back to first touch when none exist. Weights per order sum to 1.
73. Ad campaigns join attribution by `campaign_key`: BEAM's UTM convention is `utm_source = meta|google`, `utm_campaign` = the platform campaign id (Meta `{{campaign.id}}`, Google `{campaignid}`); a campaign name match is the fallback. Cost per lead = spend / persons created with that campaign as first touch; CAC = spend / people whose first purchase falls in the period with that first touch; ROAS = attributed linear revenue / spend; LTV by campaign = average `revenue_cents` of people first-touched by it.
73a. Campaign funnel (blueprint 33, query `getCampaignFunnel`): for the persons first-touched by a campaign key and created in the period, the count and share reaching each step, with the median days from lead to the step: `lead` (created), `webinar` (registered for a webinar, native or imported), `first_visit` (`first_visit_at` set), `package` (a paid order line of product kind `package`), `repeat` (`purchases >= 2`), `member` (a membership ever `active`), plus their lifetime value (sum and average `revenue_cents`) and lead cost (spend / leads). The report shows the columns campaign, source, medium, funnel steps, lead cost, first purchases, total revenue and LTV (blueprint 33's list).
74. `ads.pull_yesterday` (05:30) upserts yesterday and re-pulls the 3 days before (late conversions). After 3 consecutive failed days the digest raises a Normal alert; dashboards show "data as of <date>".
75. Marketing KPIs: leads generated = persons created in the period whose `acquisition.source` is not `import` and who had no paid order when created, split by source and campaign; registration rate = webinar registrations / delivered invitation emails of campaigns with purpose `events` linked to the webinar's session; attendance rate = registrants with live watch time > 0 / registrants (events: attended / confirmed); lead-to-visit = leads created in the period with a first visit within 90 days / leads created; visit-to-purchase = first visits in the period followed by a first purchase within 30 days / first visits; assessment completion = completed / started submissions of assessment forms; assessment conversion = completed assessments followed by a first purchase within 60 days / completed; webinar conversion = registrants with a booking or purchase within 7, 30 or 90 days, split by lead, customer and member; reactivation revenue = revenue of orders paid after a gap of more than 90 days; abandoned-cart recovery as rule 40.

**AI insights** (R4)
76. Churn and repurchase indicators are formulas, not AI (`packages/core/src/ops/predict.ts`): mean interval = (last visit - first visit) / (visits - 1) when visits >= 2, else the population median (default 30 days); `r = days_since_activity / max(14, mean interval)`; `churn = clamp(1 - exp(-0.35 * max(0, r - 1)), 0, 0.99)`, halved for active members, 0.05 with a future booking; `repurchase = clamp((1 - churn) * min(1, purchases / 3) + 0.1 * (active grant with remaining <= 2), 0, 0.99)`; `predicted_value_cents = round(avg_order_cents * purchases / max(0.25, tenure_years) * (1 - churn))`. Computed in the nightly run only while `behavioural_scoring` is approved; otherwise the columns are null.
77. NL questions: Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`) gets the `ai` schema description and the question, returns `{ sql, refusal? }` through a structured output. The server accepts only what `validateAiSql` passes: one `select` or `with ... select` statement without `;`, no comments, only relations of schema `ai`, and only functions from the allow-list `AI_SQL_FUNCTIONS` (`count, sum, avg, min, max, round, coalesce, nullif, greatest, least, lower, upper, date_trunc, extract, date_part, to_char, now, current_date, age, make_date, row_number, rank, dense_rank, lag, lead, percentile_cont, string_agg, array_agg`), so `set_config`, `current_setting`, `pg_*`, `dblink` and `lo_*` can never switch the role back or read files. A question that reads a `DPIA_GATED` relation while its DPIA is not approved is `refused`. The SQL then runs through platform's `runAiReadOnlyQuery(ctx, sql)` with `limit 200` appended when absent, then sends the column names and at most 200 rows to Claude for a short explanation in the user's locale. `person_id` columns render as links with names resolved on the server, never sent. Every call is an `ai_requests` row; the budget in `settings.ai` applies.
78. The weekly management summary (Monday 06:30) sends only aggregates (rules 34-38 KPIs for last week and the week before, retention list counts per reason) to Claude as one Message Batch (architecture section 8: Batches API for the summary, half price) with one request per locale used by owner and management users, through platform's Claude client (`createBatch`, then `getBatch` and `getBatchResults` from the follow-up job `ai.weekly_summary_collect`, which retries every 10 minutes for up to 6 hours until the batch has ended), stores the texts in `management_summaries`, and posts a link to Slack. Every request of the batch is an `ai_requests` row. It never contains person-level data.
79. Custom dashboards use only KPIs from `KPI_REGISTRY` (`packages/core/src/ops/kpis.ts`), never free SQL. A shared dashboard is visible to every user with `reports.read`; only the owner or `dashboards.manage` can edit it.

## 11. Edge cases and failure modes

1. **Nightly stats run overlaps the 5-minute refresh.** Both upsert by `person_id` with the same function; the later write wins and both results are correct. No lock beyond the row.
2. **A person is merged while stats are computed.** Merged persons are excluded; the loser's `person_stats` row is deleted by the next nightly run (rows whose person is merged or erased are removed in the same job).
3. **A refund after the period closed.** Revenue reports count the refund on its own day (rule in `v_revenue_daily`); past days never change. Person `revenue_cents` drops at the next refresh.
4. **No-show reversed by staff to attended.** The next refresh counts the visit; an inactivity event already emitted stays (append-only) but its dedupe key includes the anchor date, so thresholds re-arm from the new anchor.
5. **Cut-over import makes 8,000 people inactive at once.** Rule 16's 3-day window emits nothing for old crossings, so no inactivity burst; thresholds start firing from the day each person newly crosses one.
6. **CSV in Windows-1252 or with `;` delimiter (Excel BE).** The mapping step offers the delimiter; non-UTF-8 bytes are detected (invalid sequences) and the import fails at parse with `PermanentError` "save as CSV UTF-8"; nothing is written.
7. **The same file uploaded twice.** `import_external_ids` and the `lower(email)` match make the second run report `updated` or `skipped`; package grants use key `import:<import_id>:row:<n>`, and a second import of the same packages file is blocked when its sha256 (from `files.sha256`) equals a `done` packages import, unless the user ticks "this is a different file".
8. **Mailchimp export says "subscribed" for an address that unsubscribed in SimplyBook.** Suppressions import first (cut-over order); the consent row is `skipped` with `suppressed` (rule 47). The acceptance test "suppression survives import" covers it.
9. **A chunk job dies mid-chunk.** Each chunk is one transaction, so nothing partial commits; the retry skips rows already done.
10. **Reconciliation mismatch.** Sign-off is still possible but requires a note; mismatched rows stay listed, and staff fix balances with credits' `adjust` entries (reason mandatory), then re-open the report, which recomputes live.
11. **Erasure requested for a person with a running membership and 6 credits left.** Warnings are shown; after acknowledgement the credits module's erase handler cancels the membership and revokes the grants; invoices stay with `person_id` null; the report lists them under `retained`.
12. **Billit is down during erasure.** `privacy.erase_external` retries with backoff (up to about 7.5 hours over 6 attempts), then records `failed`; local erasure continues; the request shows "manual follow-up: Billit" and the digest lists it.
13. **Erasure crashes in the middle (step `commerce`).** Steps before it are committed and recorded; the job retries the failed step, whose handler is idempotent (deleting already-deleted rows returns 0). The report records the final row counts per step.
14. **The subject signs in during the 24-hour window and cancels.** Status becomes `cancelled`; the `privacy.erase` job runs at its time, sees `cancelled` and exits without changes.
15. **An erased person re-registers with the same email.** crm creates a new person; the suppression hash with reason `erased` blocks marketing until the person grants consent again through a form, which crm handles; `ops` treats them as a new lead with fresh stats.
16. **A new module table references `persons.id` and is not registered.** `gdpr-coverage.test.ts` fails CI listing the table and column.
17. **Retention policy points at a table with no handler.** The sweep records `no_handler`, the digest lists the key, and nothing is deleted.
18. **Ads API returns a non-EUR account.** `PermanentError` "account currency must be EUR"; that account's rows are not written; others continue.
19. **UTM campaign does not match any ad campaign id.** Revenue appears under its own campaign key with spend 0 and ROAS blank; a "campaigns without spend" table lists them so marketing fixes the UTM template.
20. **Claude writes SQL that touches `public.persons` or runs `delete`.** `validateAiSql` rejects anything but one `select`/`with`; if it slips through, `beam_ai_ro` has no grant on `public` and the transaction is read-only, so Postgres refuses; the question is stored `refused`.
21. **NL query runs longer than 5 s.** `statement_timeout` cancels it; status `timeout`; the user sees "This question is too broad; add a period or limit".
22. **AI budget exhausted.** `ask` returns `budget_exceeded` immediately; the weekly summary job ends `skipped_budget` and the Slack message says so.
23. **Weekly list on a week with fewer than 25 candidates.** The list has as many items as candidates; `size` stores the real count.
24. **A retention item's person is erased during the week.** The erase handler deletes the item; the list shows the remaining ranks with a gap, and the rank uniqueness stays valid.
25. **Export of 60,000 persons.** 12 parts of 5,000 rows; the zip streams from the database without loading all parts in memory; download links expire after 7 days.
26. **Digest job fails.** It retries with backoff; `ops_state.digest_last_sent_on` stops a double post after a late success; the missing digest appears as a dead job alert.
27. **Tester persons.** Excluded from every report and list, included in `v_person_profile` with `is_tester = true` so tester segments work.
28. **Daylight saving change.** All period boundaries are computed in SQL with `at time zone 'Europe/Brussels'`, never in server-local time; no daily or weekly `ops` schedule runs between 02:00 and 03:00, so the skipped and repeated hour never drops or doubles one of them. `ops.refresh_recent_stats` (every 5 minutes) may skip or repeat runs in that hour; it is cursor-based, so a skipped run is caught up by the next and a repeated one finds nothing new.
29. **Two staff members start a real import at the same moment.** The partial unique index `imports_one_running` lets one `startImport` commit; the other gets `conflict` "Another import is running".
30. **Staff change the mapping after a dry run and run again.** The new dry run has `run_seq + 1`, so its job keys are new and none is swallowed by the earlier run's keys; `startImport` compares the mapping hash with the latest finished dry run.
31. **Manual "Rebuild" while the Monday job builds the list.** Both take the `retention_lists` row lock; the second waits and then replaces only untouched items, so no item is lost and no task duplicated.
32. **A person erased by retention had a valid Mollie mandate.** `privacy.erase_external` revokes it through commerce before local erasure, so no renewal is charged to an anonymised profile.
33. **An old SimplyBook export still contains an erased person.** The row is skipped with `erased` (rule 47a); the person is not re-created.
34. **The centre is closed for a three-week summer break.** Retention tasks are due on the next open day (`nextBusinessDay`); credits extends package expiry through its bulk extension (credits rule 32a), which also moves `package_expiring` rows out of the list.

## 12. Build packages

## 13. Decisions made

**Release placement**
1. The views behind R2 screens (`v_retention_candidates`, `v_no_show_report`, `v_abandoned_checkouts`) ship in V1 because the architecture lists them as V1 views and the V1 action dashboard reads `v_retention_candidates`; their screens wait for R2 behind `ff_ops_retention`. R3 screens use `ff_ops_marketing`, R4 screens `ff_ops_ai`.
2. V1 "basic reporting" is the revenue report (periods with comparison, sales mix); the full management dashboard is R3 as in the proposal.
3. R3 and R4 tables are created in ops-P1 (expand-only, empty) so no later package edits `ops.ts`.
4. Email and webinar performance summaries are R3 marketing-dashboard content; per-campaign stats screens stay with the email module.

**Additions to the architecture's ops data model**

**Rules decided where the inputs were open**
13. `b2c_stage` is derived only (rules 8-13); staff correct commercial status through crm's `status_override`, never the stage. The board is read-only for stage.
14. A visit is an attended day or night booking; events count as `events_attended` and drive the `experienced` stage. No-shows never count (blueprint 48).
15. Revenue is net of refunds, includes imported history, and excludes company orders from person revenue.
16. Inactivity thresholds emit only when crossed within the last 3 days, and only the highest per window, so migrations and missed nights cause no bursts.
17. Retention scores use fixed base weights per reason plus value (1 point per EUR 200, max 30) and urgency (max 20); the weekly list is a stable Monday snapshot with a 14-day task cooldown.
18. High value means revenue of at least EUR 1,000 or the tag `vip`; both are settings.
19. Action dashboard counts are live queries, not cached; the target is under 1.5 s.
20. Previous-period comparison: day vs previous day, ISO week vs previous week, month-to-date vs the same days of the previous month, YTD vs the same span last year.
21. Customer definitions for the management dashboard use a 90-day activity window and 365 days for lost.
22. All dashboards and reports, including revenue, need `reports.read`; `reports.finance` stays for finance's own screens.

**Imports**
23. Historical purchases, visits, no-shows, webinar and assessment history import into `imported_history` and the timeline instead of fake orders, bookings or submissions, so no invoices, ledger entries or automations fire for the past.
24. Uploads go in 3 MB parts (Vercel's 4.5 MB request limit, 10 MB per `files` row), max 50 MB per import.
25. Matching is external id, then `lower(trim(email))`, then E.164 phone plus last name; existing values are kept unless overwrite is chosen; `locale` and `acquisition` are never overwritten.
26. A real run requires a dry run with the same mapping; re-importing an identical packages file is blocked by its sha256.
27. Imported memberships have no mandate; credits' payment-link renewal covers them. Only future bookings on materialised sessions import as bookings.
28. Sensitive fields are not importable; target lists contain none.
29. The cut-over order starts with suppressions; consents and persons cannot run before it without an explicit confirmation.

**Exports and privacy**
30. ZIP files are written by an in-house store-only zip writer (`zipStore`), so no new dependency is needed; CSV writing is in-house; parsing uses the installed `csv-parse`.
31. Staff exports never include sensitive columns, secrets or restricted persons; the full export is CSV per table plus a README, and the complete copy is the encrypted `pg_dump` from `backup.yml`.
32. The subject's export includes sensitive data, is encrypted at rest with `SENSITIVE_DATA_KEY_V1`, expires in 7 days and downloads only through authenticated routes.
33. Privacy requests are due in 30 days; customer requests are verified by the signed-in session; staff requests need a verification note.
34. Erasure waits 24 hours after verification (cancel window), runs external propagation first, then modules in `ERASE_ORDER`, each step its own job and transaction; an external provider that keeps failing does not block local erasure and is flagged for manual follow-up.
35. The erasure confirmation email is sent at verification, because the address no longer exists afterwards.
36. Restriction is a `processing_restrictions` row that removes the person from `v_person_profile` (and so from segments, campaigns and AI), withdraws marketing consents and exits automations; contractual processing continues. Objection adds a suppression and no restriction.
37. Rectification stores field names only; crm's audit keeps the values; finance resyncs Billit.
38. `privacy_requests` rows are kept 5 years after erasure as accountability evidence; they contain no personal data.
39. `marketing_profile` retention runs the normal erasure pipeline as `requested_by = system`, at most 200 per night, skipping people with future bookings, memberships or credits.
40. Invoices are seeded at 3653 days (10 years from the end of the fiscal year), because the Belgian law of 20 November 2022 requires 10 years for invoices and accounting documents from 2023 onwards; this deliberately supersedes the architecture's 7-year default, and finance's handler enforces the 10-year floor. Booking rows stay with the profile (booking's own policies) and lose identity at erasure or profile anonymisation; ops seeds no `bookings` key.
41. Suppression hashes and consent records are `keep` policies: documented, never swept.
41a. One owner per retention key. ops seeds its own keys and the cross-cutting ones (section 9); modules seed theirs from their own seed or declare them in `privacy.ts` (`retention`), always `on conflict (key) do nothing`. Whole-profile erasure has exactly two keys, both ops: `marketing_profile` (3 years, candidates from crm's `listInactiveProfiles`) and `marketing_contact` (leads, 2 years). crm's proposed `crm.inactive_profile` is folded into `marketing_profile`. Platform's keys keep platform's names (`audit_log` 1826, `exports`, `rendered_emails`, `jobs`).
41b. Every importer skips addresses whose suppression hash has reason `erased`, so an erased person is never re-created by a later import.
42. DPIA support is an in-app register of the six processing activities of blueprint 66; `sensitive_assessment_processing` (V1: forms with sensitive fields and the sensitive-AI switch), `behavioural_scoring`, `ai_customer_ranking` and `watch_time_profiling` gate features until approved or marked not required. The AI documentation of blueprint 61 is platform's `AI_FEATURES`; ops shows it and keeps no copy.

**R3 and R4**
43. Linear multi-touch over a 90-day lookback is the multi-touch model; first touch comes from `persons.acquisition`.
44. Ads join on the platform campaign id carried in `utm_campaign`, with campaign name as fallback; only EUR ad accounts are supported; each pull re-reads the 3 previous days.
45. Churn, repurchase and predicted value are transparent formulas (rule 76), not machine learning and not Claude ("rules before AI"); trained models are Later.
46. NL questions send Claude only the `ai` schema description, then up to 200 result rows of allow-listed columns; person names are resolved locally and never sent. Platform owns the role `beam_ai_ro`, the `ai` schema and `runAiReadOnlyQuery`; ops owns the content of its `ai` views and `validateAiSql`, which adds a function allow-list on top of platform's statement check, because `set local role` alone can be undone by `set_config('role', ...)`.
47. The weekly summary uses the Message Batches API as the architecture states (one batch, one request per locale, half price), collected by a self-re-enqueuing job every 10 minutes; it holds aggregates only and is posted to Slack as a link, not emailed.
48. Custom dashboards pick from `KPI_REGISTRY`; there is no free SQL outside the R4 question feature.
49. `ops` owns `packages/integrations/src/ads/`; it uses the Billit and Resend clients owned by finance and email and the AI gateway owned by platform.
50. Names of other modules' API functions in section 10 are the ones `ops` codes against; where an owner's spec names one differently, the owner's name wins and `ops` adapts during the Wave 2 seam work.

**Added in review**
51. Imports, privacy requests, data exports and weekly summaries have explicit state machines (rules 43a, 59a, section 2.1, the `data_exports` states in ops-P6) with one test per transition.
52. Every import job key carries `imports.run_seq`, ads pull keys carry the run date, and the manual retention rebuild runs inside its action, so no legitimate rerun is swallowed by the unique `jobs.idempotency_key`.
53. Concurrency: one real import at a time (`imports_one_running`), `stats_cursor` taken with `for update skip locked`, the weekly list row locked by build and rebuild, card and retention task creation under a row lock, erasure waits for any other running request of the person.
54. Real imports in production wait for platform's go-live gate (blueprint 77); the gate is the first cut-over step.
55. The action dashboard's webinar card works in V1 from imported webinar-tool registrations; the dashboard has a Today / This week view.
56. Revenue is shown VAT included by default with an excl. VAT toggle; prepaid credits outstanding (deferred revenue) are shown from credits' views on the V1 revenue report.
57. "Member / high-value customer" (blueprint 20): `member` follows membership; high value is the `is_high_value` flag and a badge, never a second stage.
58. Stage thresholds (90, 365, 90 days) and the no-show pattern (3 in 90 days) are settings in `retention_defaults`.
59. Task due dates use the next business day: open per `business_hours`, not a closed day, not a Belgian public holiday.
60. Customers can request export, erasure, restriction, objection and rectification from the portal; restriction and objection apply at once.
61. The subject export README carries the GDPR article 15 information (purposes, recipients, retention, source, the Belgian DPA) and large exports are stored in parts.
62. Erasure revokes valid payment mandates through commerce before local steps, and covers `site` (terms acceptances, cookie consents).
63. The daily digest adds failed automation runs and the platform and finance digest sections, so blueprint 72's monitored failures are in one daily message.
64. The campaign funnel of blueprint 33 and the "leads generated" KPI of blueprint 34 are explicit (rules 73a, 75).
65. Admin charts have table equivalents and never rely on colour alone (WCAG 2.1 AA).
